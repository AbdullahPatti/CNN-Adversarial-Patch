\begin{abstract}
Models that run on edge devices are usually compressed, and they also operate in the physical world, where adversarial
patches are the most practical attack. We study how compression changes patch robustness, and in particular
\emph{certified} patch robustness, which earlier studies of compression have not measured. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 we
compare quantization (FP16, INT8, INT4), unstructured pruning, knowledge distillation and a dense control with the same
number of parameters, at nominal compression ratios of 2\x, 4\x\ and 8\x. Every model is trained with \R{nseeds} seeds and
compared with paired statistics, and the 4\x\ arms are repeated on GTSRB, on MobileNetV2 and on ImageNette at 224\,px with
ResNet-50. We report four findings. First, compression that keeps the trained weights (quantization and pruning) also keeps
the certified accuracy of PatchCleanser: on CIFAR-10 every such model stays within \R{keepworst5}\pp\ of the defended
baseline (\R{cert5def:baseline_resnet18}\% certified for a patch covering 2.4\% of the image), whereas a smaller dense
network trained from scratch loses up to \R{d:cert5def:dense_small_8x-baseline_resnet18:abs}\pp, several times its loss in
clean accuracy. Second, empirical attacks rank the models in the same order, although the differences are smaller. Third,
weight-preserving compression also keeps the attack surface of the full model: patches made on the public full-precision
model fool a quantized deployment almost as often as white-box patches, while patches transfer poorly to and from
retrained small models. Fourth, at the same number of non-zero parameters, pruned models certify
\R{d:cert5def:prune_8x-dense_small_8x}\pp\ more images than dense ones at 8\x; the cost therefore comes from lost capacity,
not from sparsity. Across 39 CIFAR-10 models, accuracy under a random occluding patch predicts certified accuracy
(Spearman $\rho=$ \R{why:defended:occnoise8mean:cert5def}) better than clean accuracy does, and prediction agreement with the
full model predicts transfer ($\rho=$ \R{why:transfer:agreetest:tl5ratecomptofull}). ImageNette is an exception to the
first and fourth findings: the full ResNet-50 is too large for this small dataset, and the smaller models are both more
accurate and better certified.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Adversarial patches \citep{brown2017adversarial,karmon2018lavan} are a threat model that carries over to the physical
world: a printed sticker covers a small region of the image, its pixel values are unconstrained, and it can fool a
classifier wherever it is placed. Certified defenses such as PatchCleanser \citep{xiang2022patchcleanser} turn robustness
into a guarantee; for a certified image, no patch of a given size at any position can change the prediction. The systems
that most need such guarantees (cameras, drones, vehicles and embedded sensors) are also the ones whose models are
compressed before deployment, by quantization, by pruning or by distillation into a smaller network
\citep{han2016deep,jacob2018quantization,hinton2015distilling}. However, PatchCleanser was evaluated on full-size classifiers
only. Prior work on compression and adversarial robustness studies $\ell_p$ or universal perturbations
\citep{ye2019adversarial,sehwag2020hydra,matachana2021robustness}, or patches on quantized models without certification,
controls or repeated seeds \citep{guesmi2024exploring}. A practitioner who compresses a defended model therefore cannot
tell from the literature whether the guarantee survives.

We designed the study so that differences between models can be attributed to the compression method:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Matched compression.} Four families at nominal 2\x, 4\x\ and 8\x\ (13 models); the storage size of every
model is reported next to its nominal ratio (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
\item \textbf{A capacity control.} For each pruned network we train a dense network with the same number of parameters
(``dense-small'') from scratch with the baseline recipe. Comparing pruned with dense-small models separates the effect of
sparsity from that of capacity (RQ4), and comparing distilled with dense-small models shows what the teacher adds.
\item \textbf{Both attack types and both directions of transfer.} We use universal patches \citep{brown2017adversarial}
and per-image LaVAN patches \citep{karmon2018lavan}. Quantized models are attacked through a straight-through estimator
and with backward-pass differentiable approximation (BPDA) \citep{athalye2018obfuscated}, and transfer is measured over the
full $13\times 13$ matrix of models.
\item \textbf{Certification on a defended zoo.} Vanilla classifiers are almost uncertifiable
(\R{cert5vanilla:baseline_resnet18}\% for a 2.4\% patch). We therefore also build a defended zoo, trained with mask
augmentation and compressed afterwards, in the same way a deployed model would be built.
\item \textbf{Statistics.} Three training seeds, paired comparisons with 95\% $t$-intervals over seeds, and image
bootstraps for certification. We make a claim only when the interval excludes zero.
\item \textbf{Generality.} The 4\x\ arms are repeated on GTSRB \citep{stallkamp2012man} (3 seeds), on MobileNetV2
\citep{sandler2018mobilenetv2} (3 seeds) and on ImageNette at 224\,px with ResNet-50 (1 seed). In addition, two attack
variants (an untargeted universal patch and LaVAN with a margin loss) test whether the conclusions depend on the
attack's objective.
\end{itemize}

The research questions and our short answers are as follows.
\textbf{RQ3 (main question): is certified patch robustness retained after compression?} Yes for quantization and pruning in
every setting we tested. A smaller dense network loses certified accuracy in the three 32\,px settings, even on GTSRB,
where its clean accuracy is unchanged; on ImageNette it certifies more (Section~\ref{sec:cert}).
\textbf{RQ1: how does compression change empirical patch vulnerability?} The families rank in the same order as for
certification, with smaller and noisier effects (Section~\ref{sec:attacks}).
\textbf{RQ2: do patches transfer between full and compressed models?} Almost fully to quantized models, partly to pruned
models and weakly to and from retrained small models (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}).
\textbf{RQ4: does the cost come from sparsity or from capacity?} From capacity (Section~\ref{sec:rq4}).
Section~\ref{sec:why} relates these outcomes to model properties that can be measured without an attack; these properties
also account for ImageNette, the one setting where capacity reduction helps.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Patch attacks and certified defenses.} \citet{brown2017adversarial} introduced universal patches that can be
printed and placed in a scene, and LaVAN \citep{karmon2018lavan} optimizes a small patch for each image. Certified defenses
bound the effect of any patch of a given size. Existing approaches use interval bound propagation
\citep{chiang2020certified}, (de)randomized smoothing over image bands or blocks
\citep{levine2020randomized,salman2022certified}, small receptive fields with secure aggregation
\citep{xiang2021patchguard}, and double masking in PatchCleanser \citep{xiang2022patchcleanser}. PatchCleanser does not
depend on the architecture, so it can be applied unchanged to any compressed classifier. PatchCURE
\citep{xiang2024patchcure} studies the trade-off between certified robustness and the \emph{inference} cost of the defense
itself; in contrast, we keep the defense fixed and compress the classifier underneath it.

\paragraph{Compression and adversarial robustness.} Most work in this area concerns $\ell_p$ robustness and adversarial
training: pruning of robust networks \citep{ye2019adversarial,sehwag2020hydra}, sparsity as a source of robustness
\citep{liao2022achieving}, and a benchmark showing that the conclusions depend strongly on attack strength
\citep{piras2024adversarial}. Distillation can transfer robustness from teacher to student
\citep{goldblum2020adversarially}, and compression also changes \emph{which} inputs a model gets wrong
\citep{hooker2019compressed}. The closest studies to ours are the following. \citet{matachana2021robustness} study universal
perturbations (not patches) on pruned and quantized CIFAR-10 and SVHN models, and report weak transfer between pruned and
full models and possible gradient masking under quantization. \citet{guesmi2024exploring} show that patch attacks remain
effective, and transfer, across quantization bit-widths. \citet{gritsch2024preserving} find that data augmentation during
pruning preserves robustness to universal patches. None of these studies measures certified patch robustness, includes
distillation or controls for capacity at matched size, and none reports variation over training seeds.

\paragraph{Transferability.} \citet{demontis2019adversarial} explain the transfer of evasion attacks by the alignment of
input gradients between the source and the target model. We test this explanation across compression families, together
with prediction agreement and representation similarity \citep{kornblith2019similarity}.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Main setting.} We use CIFAR-10 \citep{krizhevsky2009learning} and ResNet-18 \citep{he2016deep} with a 3\x3 stem
and no max-pooling, trained for 200 epochs with SGD, Nesterov momentum and a cosine schedule. Normalization is a layer
inside every model, so attacks and masks act on raw $[0,1]$ pixels. The baseline reaches \R{clean:baseline_resnet18}\% clean
accuracy (mean over seeds 42--44). LaVAN and certification are evaluated on a fixed subset of 1{,}000 test images, and
universal patches on the full test set.

\paragraph{Compression arms} (Table~\ref{tab:models}).
\emph{Quantization}: batch normalization is folded into the convolutions, weights are quantized symmetrically per channel
and activations asymmetrically per tensor. We use FP16 (2\x), INT8 post-training quantization with 20 calibration batches
(4\x), and INT4 weights with 8-bit activations after 15 epochs of quantization-aware training (8\x). Quantization is
simulated (fake quantization) with a straight-through estimator \citep{bengio2013estimating}, so attacks can differentiate
through it.
\emph{Pruning}: global unstructured magnitude pruning of all convolution and linear weights at 50\%, 75\% and 87.5\%
sparsity in one shot, followed by 40 epochs of fine-tuning with fixed masks.
\emph{Dense-small}: ResNet-18 with widths 45, 32 and 23 (2.02\x, 3.99\x\ and 7.72\x\ fewer parameters), trained from scratch
with the baseline recipe; at each ratio it has the same number of non-zero parameters as the pruned model.
\emph{Distillation (KD)}: the same three widths, trained from the baseline teacher with temperature 4 and a weight of 0.9 on
the soft loss \citep{hinton2015distilling}.
The arms are matched on the nominal ratio. Storage size counts convolution and linear weights at their bit-width, other
parameters at 32 bits and, for pruned models, a mask of one bit per weight; 87.5\% sparsity thus stores at
\R{ratio:prune_8x}\x\ rather than 8\x.

\paragraph{Attacks.} Patches are squares with sides of 5\,px (2.44\% of the image) and 8\,px (6.25\%), pasted at random
positions. \emph{Universal}: one targeted patch per class (all 10 targets, none selected), optimized with Adam (learning rate
0.02, 1{,}000 steps, batch size 128) and expectation over location on the training set. We report accuracy with the patch
(``U5'' and ``U8 acc'') and the target hit rate averaged over targets. A random-noise patch of the same size serves as an
occlusion-only control (``Noise8'').
\emph{LaVAN}: for each image, 100 signed-gradient steps of size 0.05 and up to 10 restarts at new locations, both untargeted
(robust accuracy, ``L5'' and ``L8'') and targeted to a fixed, randomly chosen wrong class. The hyper-parameters were tuned on
the baseline: learning rates from 0.005 to 0.2 reach the same hit rate by step 250, and for LaVAN, location restarts
increase the attack's strength while extra steps do not.
\emph{Quantized models} are attacked twice, once through the straight-through estimator and once with BPDA through their
FP32 copy. For each model, patch size and mode we report the stronger of the two attacks; we do not take a per-image union,
which would give quantized models twice the attack budget. A physical variant with expectation over transformations
(rotation of $\pm 20^\circ$, brightness and contrast between 0.8 and 1.2) is reported as an ablation
(Appendix~\ref{app:physical}).

\paragraph{Certification.} We use PatchCleanser with $k=6$ mask positions per axis (36 masks of 9\,px for the 5\,px patch
and 25 masks of 12\,px for the 8\,px patch), filled with the dataset mean; we checked exhaustively that the masks cover
every patch position. \emph{Vanilla} certification uses the models above. For the \emph{defended zoo}, the baseline is
retrained with mask augmentation (each training image receives zero, one or two masks from the union of both mask sets,
with equal probability). Every compression arm is then built from this model, and augmentation stays on in every
fine-tuning, quantization-aware training and distillation run. The order is thus defend first and compress second, as for
a deployed model.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Every number is a mean over three training seeds (42--44). Attack and mask seeds are fixed, so
the seeds are paired. A comparison reports the mean of the per-seed differences with a 95\% $t$-interval ($n=3$, two
degrees of freedom), and we call it significant (bold in the tables) only when the interval excludes zero. For
certification we also give a paired bootstrap over the 1{,}000 images. We do not correct for multiple comparisons;
however, with $n=3$ the $t$-intervals are wide, which makes the claims conservative in the other direction.

\paragraph{Second settings.} For each second setting we train the baseline and the four 4\x\ arms (INT8, 75\% pruning,
dense-small and KD), in vanilla and defended versions, and run every attack, transfer and certification experiment.
\textbf{GTSRB}: ResNet-18 at 32\,px, 43 classes, 100 epochs and no horizontal flips (a flip can change the meaning of a
sign); universal patches for 10 fixed target classes; 3 seeds.
\textbf{MobileNetV2} on CIFAR-10: 120 epochs; 3 seeds.
\textbf{ImageNette} \citep{howard2019imagenette} at 224\,px: ResNet-50 trained from scratch, since ImageNet pretraining
would leak the classes and could not be shared by the small models; patch sides of 35 and 56\,px, which cover the same
areas as 5 and 8\,px at 32\,px; LaVAN and certification on 500 images; one seed.
In each setting the small widths are chosen to store about 4\x\ fewer bits. Seeds 43 and 44 and all second settings use
the checkpoint from the final epoch, so the test set plays no part in training.

\paragraph{Weight-preserving compression keeps the certified accuracy.} Across FP16, INT8, INT4 and all three pruning

\paragraph{Capacity reduction loses certified accuracy, and the loss is larger than the loss in clean accuracy.}
Dense-small models lose \R{d:cert5def:dense_small_2x-baseline_resnet18:abs},
\R{d:cert5def:dense_small_4x-baseline_resnet18:abs} and \R{d:cert5def:dense_small_8x-baseline_resnet18:abs}\pp\ of 5\,px

clean accuracy at $r=$ \R{corr:defclean:cert5def}, but the slope is steep; certification is the first property that a
smaller model loses.

\section{Empirical patch attacks (RQ1)}
\label{sec:attacks}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_attacks.pdf}

\label{fig:attacks}

Empirical attacks rank the families in the same order as certification, but the effects are smaller relative to the
variation between seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:attacks}, Table~\ref{tab:attacks}).

\paragraph{Quantization and pruning are neutral or slightly better than the baseline.} Universal 8\,px patches leave the
baseline at \R{u8acc:baseline_resnet18}\% accuracy. No quantized or pruned model is significantly worse, and 75\% pruning is
significantly better (\R{d:u8acc:prune_4x-baseline_resnet18}\pp\ \ci{d:u8acc:prune_4x-baseline_resnet18}). INT4 raises the
8\,px target hit rate by \R{d:u8hit:quant_8x_int4-baseline_resnet18}\pp\ \ci{d:u8hit:quant_8x_int4-baseline_resnet18}, and
it is the only weight-preserving arm with a significant cost. The BPDA attack through the FP32 copy never exceeded the
straight-through attack by enough to change these results, and on INT4 the straight-through attack was the stronger of the
two; we therefore see no sign of gradient masking.

\paragraph{Capacity reduction is worse.} Under the 8\,px universal patch, dense-small 4\x\ and 8\x\ lose
\R{d:u8acc:dense_small_4x-baseline_resnet18:abs} and \R{d:u8acc:dense_small_8x-baseline_resnet18:abs}\pp\ of accuracy and
KD 8\x\ loses \R{d:u8acc:kd_8x-baseline_resnet18:abs}\pp. Part of this loss is due to occlusion alone, since the same models
lose \R{d:noise8acc:dense_small_8x-baseline_resnet18:abs}\pp\ (dense-small 8\x) and
\R{d:noise8acc:kd_8x-baseline_resnet18:abs}\pp\ (KD 8\x) under a random-noise patch.

\paragraph{LaVAN.} Per-image patches are much stronger. At 8\,px no model keeps more than \R{l8max}\% robust accuracy, so
only the 5\,px patch separates the models. With the 5\,px patch, dense-small 8\x\ is the most vulnerable model
(\R{l5robust:dense_small_8x}\% robust accuracy against \R{l5robust:baseline_resnet18}\% for the baseline), and dense-small
models are the easiest to steer to a chosen class (4\x: targeted hit rate \R{d:l5tgthit:dense_small_4x-baseline_resnet18}\pp\
\ci{d:l5tgthit:dense_small_4x-baseline_resnet18}). The results for distillation are mixed: KD 2\x\ is slightly \emph{more}
robust than the baseline (\R{d:l5robust:kd_2x-baseline_resnet18}\pp), while KD 8\x\ is less robust
(\R{d:l5robust:kd_8x-baseline_resnet18}\pp).

\paragraph{Second settings.} On GTSRB the baseline already resists the universal patch well
(\R{gtsrb:u8acc:baseline}\%), and the differences between arms are small; only pruning differs significantly from the
baseline, and it is more robust (\R{gtsrb:d:u8acc:prune_4x-baseline}\pp\ \ci{gtsrb:d:u8acc:prune_4x-baseline}). On
MobileNetV2, INT8 is slightly \emph{more} robust than the baseline (\R{mbv2:d:u8acc:quant_4x_int8-baseline}\pp\
\ci{mbv2:d:u8acc:quant_4x_int8-baseline}). On ImageNette the small models are more robust to the universal patch
(dense-small \R{inette:d:u8acc:dense_small_4x-baseline}\pp, KD \R{inette:d:u8acc:kd_4x-baseline}\pp, one seed), in line
with their certification results. Distillation raises LaVAN robust accuracy on GTSRB
(\R{gtsrb:d:l5robust:kd_4x-baseline}\pp\ \ci{gtsrb:d:l5robust:kd_4x-baseline}) and lowers the targeted hit rate on
MobileNetV2 (\R{mbv2:d:l5tgthit:kd_4x-baseline}\pp\ \ci{mbv2:d:l5tgthit:kd_4x-baseline}). The smoother training signal from
the teacher may make the student harder to steer, but the effect is not consistent enough across settings to support a
claim.

\paragraph{The conclusions do not depend on the attack's objective} (Appendix~\ref{app:variants}). An untargeted universal
patch, which maximizes the loss of the true class, ranks the 13 CIFAR-10 models in the same way as the targeted patch
(Spearman $\rho=$ \R{j9:rank:u8accuu8acc} at 8\,px and \R{j9:rank:u5accuu5acc} at 5\,px) and widens the gap for dense-small
(\R{j9:d:uu8acc:dense_small_4x-baseline_resnet18}\pp\ \ci{j9:d:uu8acc:dense_small_4x-baseline_resnet18} at 4\x). LaVAN with
the Carlini--Wagner margin loss \citep{carlini2017towards} instead of cross-entropy ranks the 4\x\ arms in the same order
($\rho=$ \R{j9:rank:l5robustlm5robust}), and its robust accuracies are within a point of those of the cross-entropy attack.

\section{Transfer between full and compressed models (RQ2)}
\label{sec:transfer}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_transfer.pdf}
\caption{Transfer on CIFAR-10 in both directions: universal 8\,px target hit rate and LaVAN 5\,px untargeted transfer rate
(the share of the source model's successful patches that also fool the target, on images that both models classify
correctly). Dotted: a random-noise patch at the LaVAN locations.}
\label{fig:transfer}
\end{figure}

For deployment, the relevant attacker holds the public full-precision model and attacks the compressed version that runs
on the device. Figure~\ref{fig:transfer} and Table~\ref{tab:transfer} report this case.

\paragraph{Quantized deployments inherit the patches of the full model.} A universal patch made on the full model hits
INT8 \R{tu8hitfulltocomp:quant_4x_int8}\% of the time, compared with \R{u8hit:quant_4x_int8}\% for a white-box patch.
Per-image patches transfer less well, because LaVAN stops at the first success and leaves patches close to the decision
boundary. Even so, they transfer at \R{tl5ratefulltocomp:quant_4x_int8}\% to INT8 and \R{tl5ratefulltocomp:quant_8x_int4}\%
to INT4, far above the \R{tl5noise:quant_4x_int8}\% of a noise patch at the same location. Transfer is asymmetric: INT8
patches fool the full model \R{tl5ratecomptofull:quant_4x_int8}\% of the time. Quantization is therefore not a defense
against an attacker who uses transfer.

\paragraph{Pruned models lie in between, and retrained small models share little with the full model.} Universal patches
transfer at about \R{tu8hitfulltocomp:prune_4x}\% between the full and the 75\%-pruned model in either direction.
Dense-small and KD models are reached less often (\R{tu8hitfulltocomp:dense_small_4x}\% and
\R{tu8hitfulltocomp:kd_4x}\% at 4\x), and their own patches reach the full model even less often
(\R{tu8hitcomptofull:dense_small_4x}\% and \R{tu8hitcomptofull:kd_4x}\%). LaVAN patches from dense-small models transfer to
the full model at \R{tl5ratecomptofull:dense_small_4x}\% (4\x), close to the noise control. At equal size, pruned models
share far more of the attack surface of the full model than dense-small models do
(\R{d:tl5ratecomptofull:prune_4x-dense_small_4x}\pp\ \ci{d:tl5ratecomptofull:prune_4x-dense_small_4x} for LaVAN, compressed
$\to$ full). Distillation adds only a little shared vulnerability compared with the dense-small control
(\R{d:tl5ratecomptofull:kd_4x-dense_small_4x}\pp\ \ci{d:tl5ratecomptofull:kd_4x-dense_small_4x}).

\paragraph{The order is the same in every setting} (Table~\ref{tab:settings}): quantized $>$ pruned $>$ distilled
$\approx$ dense-small. On GTSRB, LaVAN patches from the full model transfer at
\R{gtsrb:tl5ratefulltocomp:quant_4x_int8}\% to INT8, \R{gtsrb:tl5ratefulltocomp:prune_4x}\% to the pruned model and
\R{gtsrb:tl5ratefulltocomp:dense_small_4x}\% to dense-small; on ImageNette the corresponding rates are
\R{inette:tl5ratefulltocomp:quant_4x_int8}\%, \R{inette:tl5ratefulltocomp:prune_4x}\% and
\R{inette:tl5ratefulltocomp:dense_small_4x}\%.

\section{Sparsity or capacity? (RQ4)}
\label{sec:rq4}

Pruned and dense-small models have the same number of non-zero parameters at each ratio. The difference between them is
therefore the effect of keeping a sparse subset of the trained weights instead of training a smaller dense network.
Pruned models are better certified at every ratio (Table~\ref{tab:diffs}): by \R{d:cert5def:prune_2x-dense_small_2x},
\R{d:cert5def:prune_4x-dense_small_4x} and \R{d:cert5def:prune_8x-dense_small_8x}\pp\ for the 5\,px patch and by
\R{d:cert8def:prune_2x-dense_small_2x}, \R{d:cert8def:prune_4x-dense_small_4x} and \R{d:cert8def:prune_8x-dense_small_8x}\pp\
for the 8\,px patch at 2\x, 4\x\ and 8\x, with all 8\,px intervals excluding zero. They are also more robust to the
universal patch at 4\x\ (\R{d:u8acc:prune_4x-dense_small_4x}\pp\ \ci{d:u8acc:prune_4x-dense_small_4x}) and to occlusion at
4\x\ and 8\x. The difference replicates on GTSRB (\R{gtsrb:d:cert5def:prune_4x-dense_small_4x}\pp\
\ci{gtsrb:d:cert5def:prune_4x-dense_small_4x}) and on MobileNetV2 (\R{mbv2:d:cert5def:prune_4x-dense_small_4x}\pp\
\ci{mbv2:d:cert5def:prune_4x-dense_small_4x}). Sparsity is thus not the cause of the loss; reduced capacity is, and pruning
keeps much of the trained network's capacity in the remaining weights. In agreement with lottery-ticket results
\citep{frankle2019lottery,liao2022achieving}, a pruned subnetwork does not behave like a smaller model as far as robustness
is concerned. ImageNette is again the exception, with the pruned model matching the baseline and dense-small exceeding
both.

\section{What predicts certification and transfer}
\label{sec:why}

For each of the 39 vanilla and 39 defended CIFAR-10 models, we measured properties that a practitioner can compute without
running an attack. These are accuracy with a grey or random-noise square at every position of a stride-3 grid (the mean,
the worst position, and the share of images that are correct at \emph{every} position), the logit margin, the norm and
spatial concentration of the input gradient, and similarity to the baseline of the same seed (top-1 agreement on the test
set, linear CKA of penultimate features \citep{kornblith2019similarity} and the cosine of input gradients
\citep{demontis2019adversarial}). Each (model, seed) pair is one point. We report Spearman $\rho$ with a 95\% cluster
bootstrap that resamples whole models, and the partial $\rho$ given clean accuracy, which tests whether a property carries
information beyond accuracy.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Practical guidance.} First, to deploy a certified patch defense on a small device, a defended model should be
compressed by quantization or pruning; up to 8\x\ in our experiments, this cost almost no certified accuracy. Second,
compression does not hide the model. A quantized deployment is attacked almost as well with the public FP32 weights as in
the white-box setting, and a pruned deployment is attacked substantially. Only a retrained network breaks transfer, and on
our 32\,px settings such a network is more vulnerable to white-box attacks. Third, if a smaller architecture is required,
its accuracy under occlusion should be measured before its certificate is trusted. On CIFAR-10 this measure predicted
certification better than clean accuracy, and it agreed with the certification results on MobileNetV2 and ImageNette but
not on GTSRB. Distillation recovered part of the loss on CIFAR-10.
Fourth, mask augmentation is necessary, since vanilla models are almost uncertifiable whether or not they are compressed.

\paragraph{Limitations.} This study has several limitations. First, the relation between occlusion robustness and
certification is correlational, rests on 15 to 39 models per zoo, and does not hold on GTSRB. Second, quantization is
simulated (fake quantization in floating point); integer kernels on real hardware could round differently, which we did not
measure. Third, pruning is unstructured. Structured (channel) pruning produces a narrower dense network, close to our
dense-small arm, so we expect it to behave like dense-small, but we did not test it. Fourth, ImageNette has one seed and
500 evaluation images, so its intervals reflect image sampling only. Fifth, we use one certified defense. We chose
PatchCleanser because it does not depend on the architecture, but defenses built into the architecture
\citep{xiang2021patchguard,salman2022certified} may interact differently with compression. Sixth, no model is
adversarially trained, and capacity is known to matter more for adversarially trained models \citep{sehwag2020hydra}.
Finally, seed 42 used the checkpoint with the best test accuracy (the measured gap to the final epoch is at most 0.13\pp),
whereas seeds 43 and 44 and all second settings use the final epoch, and no correction is made for multiple comparisons.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In conclusion, compression that keeps the trained weights preserves both the certified patch robustness of a defended
model and the attack surface of the full model. Compression that retrains a smaller network changes both; on our three
32\,px settings, the smaller dense networks lost certified accuracy, on GTSRB even without any loss in clean accuracy. At equal non-zero parameters, a pruned network is a
better certified model than a dense one, so the cost lies in capacity rather than in sparsity. Accuracy under random
occlusion predicted certified accuracy on CIFAR-10 and agreed with it in two of the three other settings, and agreement
with the full model predicted transfer; both are cheap checks that can be run before deployment. However, the ImageNette results show that a smaller
model can also be the more robust one when the full model is too large for its data.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All code, configurations and per-run results are released. Every number in this paper is a
macro generated from the result files, so no number was typed by hand.
