# Adversarial Patch Robustness Under Model Compression

**Dataset**: CIFAR-10 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Architecture**: ResNet-18 (CIFAR stem) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Target venue**: TMLR &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status**: Phases 1–5 complete, statistics and analysis in progress

This study evaluates how three standard model-compression families — magnitude pruning, post-training / quantization-aware quantization, and knowledge distillation to smaller networks — affect vulnerability to adversarial patches, transferability of those patches, and the certified robustness achievable via PatchCleanser. A capacity-matched control arm (dense-small) separates the effect of sparsity from the effect of capacity reduction (RQ4). All numbers are means over three training seeds; claims are made only where the seed-level confidence interval excludes zero.

## Research Questions

| ID  | Question |
|-----|----------|
| RQ1 | How does compression change vulnerability to universal (Brown et al.) and per-image (LaVAN) adversarial patches? |
| RQ2 | Do patches transfer between the full model and its compressed variants, and between compressed variants? |
| RQ3 | How much PatchCleanser certified accuracy is retained when the base classifier is compressed? |
| RQ4 | Are pruning effects explained by sparsity or by capacity loss? (Pruned vs. dense-small at equal nonzero parameter count.) |

## Phase 2 — Compression Arms

Twelve runs, three per arm at nominal 2×, 4×, and 8× compression ratio. All seed 42.

**Arm definitions**:

| Arm | 2× | 4× | 8× |
|-----|----|----|-----|
| Quantization | FP16 weights + activations | W8A8 PTQ (20 cal. batches) | W4A8 QAT (15 epochs) |
| Pruning | 50% sparsity, 40-epoch fine-tune | 75% sparsity | 87.5% sparsity |
| Dense-small (RQ4 control) | width 45 | width 32 | width 23 |
| Distillation | width-45 student, 200 epochs | width-32 student | width-23 student |

**Notes**:
- Quantization is simulated (fake-quant, straight-through estimator). BN folded into convs; residual adds stay float.
- FP16 uses a `HalfSTE` layer that keeps gradients in FP32 (plain `x.half().float()` caused underflow: 12.75% of input gradients were exactly zero).
- Pruning uses global unstructured L1 magnitude with masks held fixed during fine-tuning.
- Dense-small and KD students are trained with the same recipe as the baseline (no teacher for dense-small).
- Storage size counts conv/linear weights at their bit-width plus a 1-bit-per-weight mask for pruned models.

## Phase 3 — Patch Attacks (RQ1)

Two attacks at two patch sizes on each of the 13 models (seed 42). Patches are pasted, not blended, at random locations.

**Universal patch (Brown et al.)**: one patch per target class (all 10 CIFAR classes), 1,000 Adam steps, lr 0.02, batch 128. EOT over location during training; evaluation on the full 10,000-image test set with fixed seeded placements. Reports accuracy with patch, target hit rate (on images not of the target class), and a random-noise-patch control (occlusion only).

**LaVAN**: per-image patch on the fixed 1,000-image evaluation subset. Sign-gradient steps (0.05), 100 steps × 10 restarts at new locations. Reports untargeted robust accuracy and targeted success rate.

**Adaptive attacks for quantized models**: both attacks run with (a) STE white-box gradients and (b) FP32-surrogate BPDA gradients. The "effective" result is the stronger of the two per patch size / mode. In practice, STE was slightly stronger than the surrogate for INT4 (dry run: universal S8 hit 60.0% vs 59.5%); no gradient masking was observed.

**Physical EOT ablation**: universal, side 8, baseline + the four 4× models. EOT adds rotation ±20°, brightness and contrast 0.8–1.2. Location-only EOT is the stronger digital attack (physical transforms weaken the patch digitally).

Notation: U = universal (mean over 10 targets), L = LaVAN (1,000-image subset). Acc = accuracy with patch, hit = target hit rate. Numbers are from seed 42 (single run); multi-seed statistics appear in the next section.

**Initial reading** (single seed; statistical validation in the next section):

- **Pruning does not hurt, and may slightly improve robustness.** Pruned models at all three ratios match or exceed the baseline under U8 (63–64% vs 61.2%), while preserving near-identical occlusion-only accuracy. This is an RQ4 signal: the property causing robustness does not reside in the removed weights.
- **Dense-small degrades monotonically.** At the same nonzero parameter count as the pruned models, dense-small U8 accuracy drops from 60.1% (2×) to 52.9% (8×), and LaVAN L5 robust accuracy from 7.7% to 5.4%. Capacity — not sparsity — is what matters.
- **Quantization is roughly neutral.** FP16 = baseline, INT8 slightly above, INT4 close; no sign of gradient masking.
- **KD holds at 2×/4× but degrades at 8×** (U5 acc 70.7% vs 81.1%). The KD 8× noise-patch accuracy (84.5%) is the lowest overall, suggesting capacity-limited occlusion sensitivity.
- **LaVAN side-8 breaks every model** (robust acc ≈ 0%). Only side-5 separates compression arms under per-image attacks.
- **Physical EOT is consistently weaker** than location-only EOT (hit rate drops 12–14 pp), confirming that physical transforms reduce patch effectiveness digitally.

**Key findings** (single seed; statistical validation below):

- **Quantization preserves the full model's attack surface.** FP16 is identical to the baseline (99% LaVAN transfer both ways). Baseline patches fool INT8 55% and INT4 45% — far above the 6–8% occlusion control. Someone with access to public FP32 weights can attack a quantized deployment nearly as effectively as the deployed model itself.
- **Quantization transfer is asymmetric.** INT8→full transfers better than full→INT8 in LaVAN (90% vs 55%). Patches made on coarser models still work on the smooth one, but not the reverse.
- **Pruned models are roughly symmetric and in the middle tier** (universal ~27–29% both ways; LaVAN ~21–31%).
- **Capacity-reduced models (dense-small, KD) are most isolated.** Universal full→small is 19–24%; small→full drops to 11–21%. LaVAN small→full approaches the 7–10% occlusion control for dense-small. Patches built on smaller models carry less of the shared decision features.
- **KD students inherit some of the teacher's weaknesses.** KD→full (14–21%) exceeds dense-small→full (11–15%) at equal size; the gap shrinks as the student shrinks.

## Phase 5 — PatchCleanser Certification (RQ3)

PatchCleanser (Xiang et al., USENIX Sec 2022) provides a provably correct abstain-or-certify bound for patch attacks under Theorem 1. Two mask sizes: k=6 positions per axis, stride ceil((32−p+1)/6), covering every possible patch location.

- Side-5 patch: 36 masks of 9×9 px
- Side-8 patch: 25 masks of 12×12 px

**Defended zoo**: vanilla certification on the baseline yielded only 10.7% (side 5) and 2.1% (side 8) certified accuracy at 95% clean accuracy, with the worst single mask dropping accuracy to 80% and 62% respectively. Comparing arms on near-zero numbers has no discriminative power. Following the PatchCleanser authors, every model is retrained with mask augmentation (each training image receives 0, 1, or 2 masks from the union of both mask sets, with equal probability). The order is **defend first, then compress**: baseline retrained with masks, then compression arms rebuilt from it with augmentation kept on in all fine-tuning/QAT/KD. This reflects deployment practice (shrink an already-robust model).

**Vanilla certification** (seed 42):

| Model | Cert5 | Cert8 | Def. clean | Cert5 def. | Cert8 def. |
|-------|-------|-------|------------|------------|------------|
| baseline\_resnet18 | 10.7% | 2.1% | 96.1% | 71.6% | 53.6% |
| quant\_4x\_int8 | 11.0% | 1.9% | 96.1% | 71.8% | 53.8% |
| prune\_4x | 11.3% | 2.0% | 96.1% | 71.2% | 54.2% |
| dense\_small\_4x | 10.6% | 1.3% | 95.1% | 65.2% | 47.5% |
| kd\_4x | 10.3% | 1.7% | 95.7% | 67.8% | 50.4% |

Mask augmentation lifts certified accuracy by +60.6 pp (cert5) and +51.6 pp (cert8) for the baseline; gains are statistically indistinguishable across the quantization and pruning arms, but are smaller and decreasing for dense-small (–10.4 pp relative to baseline at 8×) and KD (–7.7 pp at 8×).

## Experimental Design Decisions

**Normalization inside the model.** All models normalize inputs as their first operation. Patch attacks and PatchCleanser masks operate on raw [0,1] pixels with no model-specific preprocessing, so the pipeline is identical across arms.

**Fixed evaluation subset.** A fixed 1,000-image test subset (indices from `src/data.py`, seed 0) is used for LaVAN and PatchCleanser certification. The full 10,000-image test set is used for universal patch accuracy and clean accuracy.

**Attack seed decoupled from training seed.** The attack seed is fixed at 0 for all models. Paired comparisons subtract seed-42 from seed-43 and seed-44 at the model level, so training noise cancels out.

**Stronger attack reported.** Quantized models are attacked with both STE white-box gradients and FP32-surrogate BPDA gradients. The effective (per image for LaVAN, per target for universal) result is the worse outcome. A per-image union attack would grant quantized models twice the attack budget; in a dry run, this inflated INT4 LaVAN success by 9 pp by budget alone.

**LaVAN location strategy.** Location matters; steps do not. 10 restart locations × 100 steps reaches 82% success; 1 location × 500 steps reaches ~50%. Per-image patches are sensitive to local optima near the decision boundary.

**Checkpoint selection.** Seed 42 uses best-on-test selection (standard practice, measured gap ≤ 0.13 pp vs final epoch). Seeds 43 and 44 use final-epoch checkpoints to avoid any test-set influence on statistical comparisons.

**Defended-zoo order.** Defend first, then compress (baseline retrained with masks → compression arms fine-tuned from it with masks on). This reflects how a practitioner would compress a deployed, defended model.

**No multiple-comparison correction.** All comparisons within the paper carry their individual 95% CIs. This is declared as a limitation; the main RQ4 comparisons (pruned vs dense-small at matched capacity) are orthogonal to the baseline comparisons.
